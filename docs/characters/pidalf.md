## Character interpretation — fictional

Working premise: a patient builder whose patience runs out at unnecessary complexity. Pidalf is a workshop wizard who dismantles machinery through blunt physical magic. Let the craft be competent and the equipment look improvised.

Proposed animation vocabulary: inspect a broken part, squint, dismiss it with a flick; lean on the staff during an enemy's extravagant windup; suddenly deliver a decisive full-body strike; straighten a crooked homemade attachment afterward. These are invented, not observed personal tics.

Visual proposal: workwear mixed with a worn wizard coat, a handmade pi-shaped staff, paper/tape/brass details. Charcoal, parchment, and warm amber separate him from Tibo's lime, Peter's coral, and Dimillian's purple. Obtain a verified face reference before drawing likeness.

### Revised working proposal: force and loop wizard

Not an approved kit; no implementation. Pidalf keeps one body and grounded movement. His specialty is choosing **where a spell originates and when its repeated casts intersect enemies**. Instant primary attacks must be satisfying without setup; repeat spells provide positioning depth.

- Primary scroll modes: a narrow snapping lightning lance (accurate direct damage); a broad, short force wave (shove/stagger); a slower rotating force ring (outbound/returning damage). These are sidegrades. Each needs its own staff pose, tip silhouette and effect. Keep continuous scroll feedback but avoid another spendable mana economy.
- E, Loop: place one visible world-space rune in range and line of sight. The next primary cast records its mode and direction; the rune repeats that spell twice with a clear beat, then expires. No target seeking, recursion or permanent turret. Placement preview should show firing direction. Replacing a rune discards the old one; shared recovery prevents spam. The purpose is crossfire and repeated force positioning, not an autonomous pet or extra character. Start with one spell before expanding modes.
- Q, Breakpoint / You Shall Not Pass: a short, fixed frontal ward arrests a bounded number of incoming bullets, visibly hanging them in place. When it ends the shots resume their original trajectories after a readable warning. Pidalf can move out of their line while they are stopped. No reflection, token conversion or permanent projectile deletion; rear and explosions remain threats. This is a tentative defensive mechanic: if resuming bullets is confusing or unfair, revise before expanding the kit.
- F, Compact: a bounded local field draws ordinary robots and loose debris together, compresses briefly, then bursts outward with physical knockback. Heavy enemies resist displacement; bosses take damage/stagger only. It should support lining up a primary/loop and keep the world readable rather than becoming a screen-wide wipe.

Example: plant a lightning rune on a ledge, cast through an approaching squad, then switch to force and shove enemies back across the rune's two remaining pulses. For survival, briefly stop a volley, move above its path, and let it pass underneath. This is the distinction to test.

Visual direction: grey cloak, exposed tied-back hair and round glasses, readable pi-shaped staff; warm-white/amber magic against the neutral world. Staff plants, cloak follow-through, physical recoil and decisive impact pauses sell wizard power. Small paper/cardboard repair details nod to Pipi; do not add a second summon system just to include every reference. Fictional low-frequency lines can include “two tools would've done it,” “stay in the loop,” or a dry “simpler.” Avoid authentic-quote claims.

## Latest user direction: continuous scale, physical compaction and force dragging

The user likes Compact and develops it into a cursor-targeted physical mechanic. Scroll should scale abilities rather than select unrelated spells. At small scale, aim at an individual enemy and compress it like a car press, leaving a little cube. At large scale, a black-hole-like effect gathers multiple enemies into a ball that can roll. The user also wants palm-forward telekinesis: hold and move one enemy or, at larger scale, a region of enemies. Moving the cursor quickly then releasing should impart momentum, rather than needing separate push/pull buttons. This supersedes the earlier Compact proposal's automatic outward explosion and the unrelated three-spell scroll roster. Still a design discussion, not an implementation request.

Proposed coherent mapping (not yet accepted): click SLOP; hold E to grab/drag, release E to throw using the recent movement of held bodies; F Compact at the cursor; scroll continuously adjusts influence size/strength. Q remains undecided. No separate force-push key. Preserve WASD, independent mouse aim and normal grounded movement.

Design proposals:

- Small Compact: a briefly outlined target is visibly crushed from multiple sides, with metal creaks, limbs folding, and an impact beat when the cube drops. Large Compact: a short gathering phase with a readable radius; bots physically spiral inward, compress into a scrap ball, and drop with weight. Cube and ball remain collidable, movable combat props for a bounded lifetime/count. Compaction is not an instant undifferentiated damage explosion.
- Telekinesis visibly holds bots rather than controlling their AI. Bodies follow the cursor with a short spring-like lag and collide with the world. Direction and speed come from actual recent body movement, with smoothed/clamped release velocity so trackpad spikes cannot create absurd speeds. Ordinary ground movement stays available. Held bots visibly dangle and struggle; larger groups feel heavier. Initial targeting needs range and line of sight; pulling cannot move targets through solid walls.
- Scale continuously changes reach of influence, number/mass budget and effect appearance. High scale trades precision/speed for area and longer commitment/recovery. It must not simply be the strongest setting with no downside. Preview what would be affected, unobtrusively in world space, and lock Compact's selected scale when its cast begins. Exact numerical tuning and whether E can gather extra targets while already held remain open.
- Natural combo: compress bots into physical ammunition, grab the result, swing and release it into another group; optionally let SLOP break a compacted object into a short scrap blast. That final interaction is an assistant proposal, not a user requirement.
- Enemy limits remain important: light bots can be fully manipulated; heavy units resist/require more commitment; bosses do not become freely draggable or instantly compactable. Keep single-target attacks useful in ordinary combat. Bound mass, duration and simultaneous props so large-area control cannot permanently remove an encounter's threats.

Character identity now centers on direct, physical editing of enemy bodies: dismiss, compress, move. Wizard clothing and a pi staff frame the actions; hands, posture and enemy deformation carry them. Pi-inspired terminology is flavor, not menus or literal programming chores. Relative to the roster: Tibo's projectile expenditure/recovery, Peter's autonomous pet placement, and Dimillian's form switching remain distinct from Pidalf's cursor-directed manipulation and persistent physical results.
